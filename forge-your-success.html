FORGE Your Success

The weekly class at the center of FORGE Successful Reentry. Each class takes one tool from FORGE and puts it to work on one part of life after release: the job, the parole office, the family table, the paycheck, the old neighborhood.

The FORGE Your Success elevator speech

Every class opens with the men saying it together. It keeps the heart of the FORGE elevator speech, in the voice of a man who is home:

“FORGE — Facilitating Opportunities for Reentry, Growth and Empowerment. I’m home, and I’m building a new life — one where I lead by example, keep my word, and make a positive impact on my family and my community. My goal is simple: to turn time served into time that serves others. My purpose: to show what a second chance looks like — through honest work, real accountability and service — and to help the next man coming home do the same.”

A book he keeps

Every man gets his own copy of FORGE Your Success: A guide for men coming home. It has a chapter for every class, written for a man reading on his own: the idea, the tool step by step, examples from life after release, the traps to watch for, pages to write in, and what to keep doing after he leaves the house. At the back: every tool on a few pages, a page for the numbers he'll need, and a reading list of books for the road.

How the class works

The Reentry Home takes residents as beds open, not as a group, so FORGE Your Success runs as a cycle of twelve weekly classes. A new resident joins at whatever class comes next; every class stands on its own.

A resident who stays about six months goes through the cycle twice: first as a participant, learning each tool and using it on his own Reentry Plan; then as a peer helper, sharing what he tried, playing the hard part in role-plays and coaching newer residents. Know yourself, build others, lead and serve: FORGE in miniature.

Every class runs 90 minutes in the same shape, because men coming home are rebuilding routine:

Opening circleOne win and one challenge since last class.
Reentry Plan checkIn pairs: one step done, one step next.
TeachThe FORGE tool, explained from the beginning, then applied to life after release.
PracticeRole-play in the FORGE simulation format: set the scene, try it, freeze, coach, replay.
Commit and closeOne action for this week, written into the Reentry Plan and said out loud.
