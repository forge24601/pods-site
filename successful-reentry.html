Reentry Home

FORGE Successful Reentry

FORGE trains men inside to lead. Successful Reentry carries the same philosophy into the first six months home: structure, accountability, service and mentorship, applied to housing, work, money, family and supervision.

Draft program design. This is a first outline for discussion with POD Society. Stages, lengths and schedules will change.

The same philosophy, a new setting

Inside, FORGE teaches men to know themselves, build others, and lead and serve. Those skills don’t stop mattering at the gate. A parole officer, a new boss, a landlord, a son who barely remembers you: the first months home are full of moments where thinking drives behavior and one bad choice can undo years of work.

Successful Reentry gives residents of the Reentry Home a clear path through those months, and keeps the habits FORGE built: catching the thought before the action, owning what you do, and serving others.

What carries over from FORGE

  • Stakeholder, not passenger. You shape what happens in your house, your job and your neighborhood.
  • Think, feel, act. Your thinking drives your feelings and your behavior. Catch the thought first.
  • Accountability. Own it fully, with no “but.”
  • Trust is earned. One honest moment at a time, with your family and your mentor.
  • Service over self. Turn time served into time that serves others.

Three stages

Each man moves through the stages at his own pace. Moving up comes with more freedom and more responsibility. Lengths are a draft.

  1. 1

    Land

    First 30 days

    • Move in; sign the house agreement and the FORGE commitment
    • Meet your mentor; write your first Reentry Plan
    • State ID, Social Security card, birth certificate, phone
    • First parole or probation check-in, and a plan for every one after
    • Health and recovery check, medication continued
    • Start job skills classes
  2. 2

    Build

    Months 2 and 3

    • Job search, then a first job and first paycheck
    • Bank account, budget and a savings goal
    • Reconnect with family, on terms that are safe for everyone
    • Weekly FORGE Your Success class
    • Keep every supervision appointment and condition
  3. 3

    Launch

    Months 4 to 6

    • Steady work and savings toward a place of your own
    • A move-out plan with a backup
    • Mentor a newer resident
    • Community service with POD Society
    • Complete the program and join the alumni circle

The Reentry Plan

Every resident writes a plan with specific actions in each area, and reviews it with a mentor every week. It builds on the five areas FORGE mentors learn to plan for in Phase 2 (housing, identification, employment, healthcare and family) and adds money, supervision and service.

Housing

Where you live now, where you’ll live next, and the backup if that falls through.

Identification

State ID, Social Security card, birth certificate. Nothing else works without them.

Work

A job now, and a path to a better one: skills, certifications, references.

Money

Banking, a budget, savings, fees and fines, and staying clear of predatory loans.

Health and recovery

Medication, mental health care, recovery support, and a doctor or clinic.

Family

Rebuilding relationships honestly and patiently, including child support and visitation.

Supervision and legal

Every check-in, every condition, and help with legal matters when they come up.

Service

Giving back: mentoring, volunteering, being a positive example in the community.

Who runs it

  • POD Society staff run the house and the program.
  • Credible messengers mentor residents and lead the house circle.
  • Certified FORGE mentors who are home serve as mentors and help teach FORGE Your Success.
  • Partners teach job skills, provide recovery and legal support, and hire residents.

What success looks like

  • Working, with savings in the bank
  • A safe place to live after the program
  • Supervision completed without violations
  • No new charges
  • Family relationships rebuilt where they can be
  • Giving back: mentoring the next resident

POD Society will track these for every resident, during the program and for a year after.

The FORGE Your Success class

The weekly class at the center of the program: twelve classes that take the skills of FORGE (thinking reports, STOP, the escalation curve, the conflict resolution model, accountability conversations, the Stages of Change, OARS) and apply them to the job, the parole office, the family table and the neighborhood.
